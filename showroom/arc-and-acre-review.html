Three sites worth learning from

The competitor review.

01 / Yardzen

Clear packages and quizzes.

Yardzen does a good job showing packages and helping visitors start with a quiz. Arc & Acre moves the visitor from an image to a short project brief without asking them to pick a large package first.

02 / Bower & Branch

Smart, local plant guidance.

Bower & Branch explains the design steps and planting expertise clearly. Arc & Acre keeps the first choice simple: garden, poolside, or small courtyard.

03 / ShrubHub

Strong visuals and pricing.

ShrubHub shows 3D transformations and package prices. Arc & Acre uses a calmer page, fewer sale messages and a useful live brief to invite premium enquiries.

The UX argument

Why this approach may work.

1. Show first.

Beautiful outdoor photos help visitors see the type of space before reading about services.

2. Choose next.

Only three main service paths reduce the number of early decisions.

3. Send a better brief.

The form gives the visitor a live summary they can copy, save or email.

These are design hypotheses. No conversion lift over any competitor has been measured. Arc & Acre is a concept business and the photos are illustrative, not completed client work.
